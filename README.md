# 📊 HR Analytics — Employee Attrition Analysis

> **Turning HR Data into Actionable Insights with Python & Jupyter Notebook**

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes the **IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition Dataset** using Python and Jupyter Notebook.

The objective is to understand employee attrition and identify patterns associated with employees leaving an organization.

## 🎯 Objectives

* Analyze overall employee attrition
* Identify departments with higher attrition
* Study overtime and employee exits
* Identify high-turnover job roles
* Compare income between leavers and stayers
* Analyze age and tenure
* Generate HR insights and retention recommendations

## 📂 Dataset

| Metric                 |  Value |
| ---------------------- | -----: |
| Total Employees        |  1,470 |
| Total Attributes       |     35 |
| Employees Who Left     |    237 |
| Employees Who Stayed   |  1,233 |
| Overall Attrition Rate | 16.12% |

## 🔎 Key Findings

* **Sales** has the highest department-level attrition at approximately **20.6%**.
* Employees working overtime leave at approximately **2–3 times the rate** of employees who don't.
* **Sales + Overtime** shows approximately **37.5% attrition**.
* **Sales Representatives** have the highest job-role attrition at approximately **39.8%**.
* Employees who left earned approximately **30% less** on average than employees who stayed.
* Younger employees and employees with shorter tenure show higher attrition patterns.

## 💡 HR Recommendations

1. Reduce excessive overtime, particularly within Sales.
2. Review compensation for junior and entry-level positions.
3. Focus retention strategies on employees during their first **3–5 years**.
4. Use HR analytics to support data-driven retention decisions.

## 📈 Business Impact

This project demonstrates how HR teams can use employee data to move from intuition-based decisions toward **data-driven workforce management**.

## 🧠 Key Takeaway

> **Young, early-tenure employees in front-line roles, particularly Sales Representatives who work overtime and earn below-average pay, show stronger patterns of employee attrition in this dataset.**
